It’s Friday afternoon. You’ve had a busy week in the showroom, there are designs to finish, and several customers are waiting for revised prices. Somewhere in your inbox is a message from a couple who loved their kitchen design but wanted to discuss a different worktop.

You meant to call them on Wednesday.

For kitchen, bathroom and bedroom businesses, this is a familiar problem. Following up takes more than remembering to send an email. You need to know what was discussed, what has changed and what the customer is waiting for. Finding that information can take longer than writing the message itself.

This is where AI can be useful. It can help your team catch up on a conversation, work out what needs attention and prepare a reply.

## Less time piecing the story together

Think about what happens before you call a customer about their quote.

You might read the last few emails, check the design notes, find the latest price and ask a colleague what was agreed during the customer’s showroom visit. If you’ve been handling other projects all week, you need that context before picking up the phone.

An AI assistant with access to the relevant project information could pull those details into a short briefing:

> The customers are happy with the layout. They asked for a price using quartz instead of laminate, which hasn’t been sent yet. Their builder is due to start in January. You agreed to call once the revised price was ready.

You can quickly see that the next step is to finish the revised price. Another email asking whether they have made a decision would be premature.

The briefing needs to link back to the original notes and messages so the salesperson can check anything unclear. Its usefulness depends on the quality of the information available.

## Follow-ups that sound like you remember the customer

People notice when a business remembers the details of their project.

A message about the worktop they liked or the budget question they raised gives them something specific to respond to. Writing that message is usually straightforward once you have the conversation fresh in your mind.

AI can help by preparing a draft from the project history:

> Hi Sarah, I’ve got the revised price for the quartz worktop we discussed. Would you and James have time for a quick call this week to go through the difference and see how it fits with your budget?

The salesperson checks the details, adjusts the wording and sends it. If the revised price isn’t ready, the draft should reflect that instead.

This is a useful place to keep a person involved. Prices, product choices and dates need to be right, and your team will often understand something about the customer that hasn’t made it into the notes.

## Picking up on what needs to happen next

Customer emails don’t arrive neatly labelled with the next action.

A short reply might contain a request for different doors, a question about fitting and a mention that the building work has been delayed. Someone has to read it, work out what it means for the project and make sure the right people know.

AI can help turn that reply into suggested tasks: ask the designer to revise the specification, get an answer about fitting and update the planned follow-up date.

That can be especially useful with a shared inbox. When several people are involved in a sale, it is easy for everyone to assume someone else has picked up a request.

Each task still needs an owner. The benefit is that the team has less information to sort through before deciding who should do what.

## Making the outstanding quote list more useful

Most showrooms have a way of tracking open quotes. The harder question is whether that list tells you what is actually happening.

A customer who is waiting for a revision needs different attention from one who has put their renovation on hold. Yet both might sit in the same “quote sent” column for weeks.

AI can help review the notes and correspondence behind those records. It could flag a question that appears unanswered, a promised call that is overdue or a customer who has asked to reconnect in a few months.

That gives a showroom manager a more useful starting point for a sales review. The team can spend the meeting discussing how to move projects forward, with less time spent asking, “Does anyone know what happened with this one?”

Simple reminders still have a role here. Once a call is agreed for Tuesday, an ordinary task reminder can handle it. AI adds value where someone would otherwise need to read through a conversation to understand the next step.

## It has to fit the way your team works

If using AI means copying every email and quote into another application, it can quickly become another job.

Start with the tools your team already uses. Where are the quotes? Where do people record customer conversations? How are design changes passed between salespeople and designers?

The answers will show what information an AI assistant needs and whether your systems can make it available. In some businesses, a connection to the inbox and customer records may be enough for a useful first step. Others may need to tidy up how they record project updates before automation will help.

There is no need to connect everything at once. Choose one task that takes up time regularly and make that easier.

## Start with the follow-up you keep putting off

A sensible first trial is helping the team prepare follow-ups for outstanding quotes.

Take a small group of enquiries. Let the assistant summarise the recent conversation and draft a message for each one. Have the salesperson review the result before anything is sent.

You’ll soon see where it helps. Perhaps it saves time on older enquiries, where nobody remembers the details. Perhaps the summaries are useful, but the drafts need too much editing. That is valuable to know before expanding the setup.

Look at the whole task when judging the result. How long does it take to prepare and check a follow-up? Are the details accurate? Is the team finding it easier to keep the promises made to customers?

The aim is to give your team more time for the conversations that move a project forward. Customers still need someone who understands their home, their budget and the choices they are making. AI can make it easier for that person to arrive at the conversation prepared.
